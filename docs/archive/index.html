Puzzle archive: every daily garden by date

Missed a day? Every puzzle from the last 30 days – counted from our launch on November 2, 2026 – is one tap away: five puzzle types, three levels each. Solved ones are marked in yellow, using the progress stored on this device.

How the archive works

Daily Logic Garden has no puzzle database. Every board is grown in your browser from three ingredients: the date in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), the puzzle type and the level. These are turned into a number – the seed – and the seed drives our generator. The same seed always produces the same sequence of random choices, so the same board grows on every device in the world.

That has a pleasant consequence: the archive does not have to store anything. The list above is computed on the spot from today’s date, going back 30 days. When you open an older day, its board is regrown from its date and checked exactly like a fresh one. The links carry the date and level in the address (for example ?date=2026-11-02&level=bloom), so you can bookmark a specific puzzle or send it to a friend.

Why 30 days

Thirty days is a deliberate limit. The archive is meant for catching up after a holiday or a busy week, not for an endless backlog. A month gives you 30 × 5 × 3 = 450 boards to choose from, which is plenty, and it keeps the list short enough to scan on a phone. Older seeds still work in principle, but the site only links the last 30 days so that everyone can find the same days in the same place.

Your progress, kept on your device

A yellow letter means you solved that board on this browser. The information comes from your garden, which lives in your browser’s local storage – we never see it. If you use a second device, its archive starts empty; you can move your garden over with the export and import buttons on the My garden page. Clearing your browser’s site data removes the garden, so export it first if you want to keep it.

Half-finished boards are saved too. If you started a Grove board last Tuesday and stopped halfway, opening it from the archive brings back your marks, your hint count and your time so far.

There is no penalty for playing old puzzles. An archive board you solve today plants its plant in your garden just like a fresh one, and your garden never loses anything because you skipped a day.

Which day is “today”?

The puzzle day changes at midnight UTC. In New York that is 7 p.m. in winter (8 p.m. during daylight saving time), in London midnight in winter and 1 a.m. in summer, in Sydney late morning. We use UTC so that there is exactly one puzzle per day worldwide; using local midnights would give people in different time zones different boards on the same calendar date.

The page reads your device’s clock and converts it to UTC. If your clock is set wrong, you may see a different day’s puzzle than everyone else. Dates in the future are never offered, and dates older than 30 days fall back to today’s puzzle.

If you would rather work through a week on paper, the printable packs use the same dates and the same boards, one puzzle per page.

Sharing an archive puzzle

Because the address contains the date and the level, you can send a friend a link to exactly the board you just played – for example a tricky Grove Garden Path from last weekend – and compare notes afterwards. The result line you copy after solving never contains the solution, only the puzzle, level, date, time and hint count.

Questions and answers

Can I play puzzles older than 30 days?

The archive links the last 30 days only. That keeps the list manageable and makes sure everyone sees the same selection.

Why is a day marked as solved on my phone but not on my laptop?

Progress is stored in each browser separately. Export your garden on one device and import it on the other to combine them.

Do archive puzzles count for my garden?

Yes. Any solved board plants its plant, whatever its date, and each board can only be planted once.

Are archive boards easier or harder than today&rsquo;s?

Neither. Each date gets its own boards from the same generator with the same level rules.

Can a puzzle in the archive change?

Only if we change the generator itself, which would be noted on the About page. The seeds are fixed, so the same date gives the same board.
