Method

What makes a logic puzzle fair? Unique solutions explained

A fair logic puzzle has exactly one solution, and you can reach it by reasoning alone. That sounds obvious, but computer-made puzzles often fail one of the two. Here is what each condition means, why it matters, and how our generator checks both for every board.

Condition 1: exactly one solution

If two answers fit, the solver eventually reaches a point where no reasoning can decide, and must pick. The end feels arbitrary, and a checker that compares against one stored answer may reject a perfectly valid solution. Uniqueness is the minimum standard.

We check it by brute force: a separate program, independent of the logic solver, searches all possible fillings of each board and must find exactly one.

Condition 2: no guessing needed

A puzzle can have exactly one solution and still require trial and error, if the only way to make progress is to try an option and follow its consequences far ahead. For a human that is tedious. Our standard: a solver using a fixed list of human techniques must finish the board. The techniques are local – the last open square, a line count, a single imagined placement checked against one line or region – and never a deep search.

Condition 3: an honest difficulty

We rate a board by the hardest technique it needs, not by its size. The solver tries techniques from easy to hard; a board is Sprout if the easy tier finishes it, Bloom if it needs the middle tier, Grove if it needs the top tier. Every published board is also checked not to be solvable one tier lower.

From date to board

The date (in UTC), puzzle type and level form a seed. The generator proposes a layout, the solver and counter test it, and the first layout that passes is the day’s board. Because the seed fixes every random choice, everybody gets the same board without a server.

Fair hints

A fair puzzle deserves fair help. Our hints never just reveal a square: they state the next deduction and why it holds. If your board contains a mistake, the hint points at it first. In our automated tests, following only hints solves every tested board, and each hint’s claim matches the solution.

Limits

Fairness is not the same as fun. A board can be fair and dull. Our minimizing generator removes givens that are not needed, which tends to make boards elegant, but taste remains a matter of opinion. Tell us via contact when a board felt wrong.

Why boards are minimized

After the generator has a valid board, it tries to remove each given square, stone or clue in turn. If the solver can still finish the board at its level without it, it stays removed. The result is a board in which every remaining piece of information is needed – for that solver. This is what makes the boards feel tight: there are no decorative givens, and each one you see contributes to some deduction. It is also why Grove boards often have fewer givens than you might expect: harder techniques extract more from less.

Minimizing is done greedily in a random order fixed by the seed, so a board is minimal in the sense that no single given can be removed – not necessarily the smallest possible board for its solution. Hand-crafted puzzles often go further and place givens to create a theme or a specific “aha” moment; that is an art our generator does not attempt.

A short checklist for any puzzle

  • Does the publisher state that the puzzle has a unique solution?
  • Can every step be explained with “because”, using only the rules and clues?
  • Does the difficulty label match what you needed?
  • Are pieces distinguishable without color for everyone who plays?

Our own answers to these questions are on the About & method page.

What a human solver adds

A computer check proves that a board is unique and solvable with a given set of techniques. It does not prove that a person will enjoy the route. That is why the hints matter: when a board needs its hardest technique, the hint names it and points at the squares involved, so the hardest moment of the puzzle becomes a moment of learning rather than frustration. And it is why we publish the technique lists openly, so you can judge our ratings for yourself.

Questions and answers

Does a unique solution mean no guessing?

No. A puzzle can be unique but require deep trial and error. We check both conditions separately.

Who checks the puzzles?

Our code does, in your browser, every time a board is generated. Automated tests run the same checks on 60 consecutive days for every puzzle and level.

Can a board be rated wrong?

The rating follows our technique list. Someone using a different method may find a board easier or harder, but it never needs anything beyond its tier.

Why not use human-made puzzles?

Human setters make wonderful puzzles. A generator lets us offer fifteen fresh boards every day with guaranteed checks and no copying.

What if I find two solutions?

Please tell us the date, puzzle and level. Our tests make it very unlikely, but we would correct it immediately.
