Getting started

Logic puzzles for beginners: where to start

Logic puzzles need no math and no trivia – only the willingness to say “because”. If you have never solved one, this page tells you which puzzle to try first, how our three levels build up, and what to do when you get stuck.

What a logic puzzle is

A logic puzzle gives you rules and a few clues. Every step follows from them – no knowledge, no luck. The pleasure is the moment a square becomes certain because of something you noticed. All five puzzles on this site have exactly one solution, so there is always a next certain step.

How the levels build up

Sprout needs one rule at a time. Bloom asks you to combine two facts – a count and a position, a clue and a grid. Grove adds a short look ahead: imagine one placement and check one consequence. Stay at Sprout until you finish without hints a few days in a row; nothing is lost by staying.

When you get stuck

Being stuck is normal and means a fact is hiding in plain sight. Look at the smallest region, the fullest line, the clue you haven’t used. Then press Why?: it shows one step and its reason, not the answer. Read it, find the pattern yourself, and apply it.

Five habits from day one

  1. Mark what is impossible, not only what is certain.
  2. Never place something “to see”. If you can’t say why, don’t.
  3. After each move, check what it changed nearby.
  4. Take breaks; fresh eyes find steps.
  5. Play daily but briefly. The garden grows anyway, and missed days cost nothing.

When you want more, the guides on Star Battle strategy and logic grids go deeper.

The rules in one line each

  • Sun & Shade: no three alike next to each other; half suns, half shades in every line.
  • Flower Beds: one flower per row, column and bed; flowers never touch.
  • Tents & Trees: each tree gets its own tent beside it; tents never touch; numbers count tents.
  • Garden Path: one line through every square, from stone 1, stones in order.
  • Who Planted What: each gardener has one crop, bed and day; use the clues.

If you remember these five lines, you can play everything on this site. The longer explanations on each puzzle page add controls, examples and the first techniques worth learning.

Why reasoning beats guessing

It is tempting to place a piece and see what happens. On a board with a unique solution, that usually leads to a contradiction many moves later, and then you don’t know which of your moves was wrong. Reasoning step by step is slower at first and much faster overall, because you never have to undo. It is also where the satisfaction lies: the solution is not found, it is proven.

After the first weeks

Once Sprout boards feel easy, move one puzzle at a time to Bloom. Each Bloom level introduces exactly one new kind of idea, and the hint will show it to you the first time you need it. Read the matching guide afterwards – Queens-style rules, Tents strategy, binary rules or logic grids – to see the idea explained with diagrams. Grove is for when Bloom boards go smoothly without hints. There is no hurry: many regular players stay at Bloom for months and enjoy every board.

Your garden records the level of every solved puzzle, with taller plants for harder levels, so you can see your own progress over the weeks without any scores or rankings.

One last tip: talk yourself through a step out loud, or explain it to someone else. If you can finish the sentence “this square must be a flower, because …”, the move is safe. If the sentence trails off, it is a guess in disguise – look again or ask for a hint.

Questions and answers

Do I need to be good at math?

No. You need to count to ten at most. Logic puzzles are about consequences, not calculations.

How long does a Sprout puzzle take?

Usually a few minutes once you know the rules; the first one may take longer.

Are hints cheating?

No. They explain a step, and our garden rewards every solved puzzle the same way.

Can children play?

The Sprout levels suit older children with an adult. Please note the site shows advertising and is designed for adults.

Do I need an account?

No. Your progress is kept in your browser.
