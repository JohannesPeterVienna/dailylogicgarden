Privacy policy

This policy explains what data is processed when you visit dailylogicgarden.com, why, on which legal basis, and which rights you have.

Controller

AdStern, Seilerstätte 8, 1010 Vienna, Austria, office@adstern.com. More details are in the imprint.

Hosting on GitHub Pages

The site is a collection of static files served by GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. To deliver a page, GitHub necessarily receives technical data from your browser, such as your IP address, the requested address, date and time, browser type and the referring page, and may store it in server logs for a limited time for security purposes. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in delivering a secure website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Data transfers to the USA are based on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

Puzzle progress in your browser

Your moves, hint counts, times, chosen level, preferences and your garden are saved in your browser’s local storage on your device so you can continue later. This data never leaves your device and is not sent to us or anyone else. You can delete it by clearing this site’s data in your browser; the export file on the “My garden” page is created on your device only. Storing this data is necessary to provide the puzzle function you request (§ 165(3) Austrian Telecommunications Act 2021).

Advertising by Google AdSense

Puzzle, guide and overview pages (the home page, the guide overview, the puzzle archive, the printable packs page and About) can show ads; Google places them automatically, so their number and position can vary. The imprint, this privacy policy, the contact page and your personal garden page load no advertising. The ads are delivered by Google AdSense, provided by Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland (and, for users outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google LLC, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA). AdSense uses cookies and similar technologies to show ads, limit how often you see an ad, measure performance and prevent fraud.

Third party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on a user's prior visits to this website or other websites. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to users based on their visit to this site and/or other sites on the Internet.

You can opt out of personalized advertising in Google’s Ads Settings, at www.aboutads.info or, in Europe, at www.youronlinechoices.eu. How Google uses information from sites that use its services is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Consent

Visitors from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland see a consent message from Google’s certified consent management platform, which follows the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework (TCF). Until you decide, and if you decline, Google Consent Mode is set to “denied” for advertising and analytics storage. Personalized ads need your consent. Non-personalized ads also use cookies or identifiers for frequency capping, reporting and fraud prevention and therefore also need consent to store and access information on your device. Without that consent Google can only show limited ads. Limited ads do not use cookies or local storage for personalised advertising; Google may still use them for fraud prevention and frequency limits as described in its documentation, and it processes technical data such as your IP address to deliver the ads. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 165(3) TKG 2021). Google’s consent platform stores your decision on your device (for example as a cookie holding the TCF consent string) so that the message does not reappear on every page. You can change or withdraw your choice at any time via “Privacy settings” in the footer of every page that shows ads; while our statistics window is in use and on pages without ads (such as this one) the button opens that window, which links to Google’s choices (see Interim statistics window). If a U.S. state privacy law applies to your visit, the button opens Google’s U.S. opt-out dialog instead, where you can opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information and of targeted advertising; after an opt-out, ad personalization and Google Analytics stay off.

Interim statistics window

At the moment Google’s consent message is set up on this site but is not displayed. So that visits are still only counted with your consent, we show our own small window while this is the case. It appears about three seconds after a page with ads has loaded if Google’s consent management has not reported a decision by then, is not displaying its own message and does not report that the GDPR does not apply to your visit. The window asks only about visitor statistics with Google Analytics (Google Ireland Limited); it is not a consent to advertising and never switches on advertising storage, ad user data or ad personalization – these stay “denied”. “Allow” and “Decline” are equal choices. Closing the window with “×” or Escape, or not choosing, stores nothing and is not consent: if no choice is saved yet, Google Analytics stays off and we ask again on the next page with ads; a choice you saved earlier stays in effect until you change it.

What is stored: your choice is saved in your browser’s local storage under the key dlg-stats, with a version number, the choice (“granted” or “denied”) and the time of your choice. It is not sent to us or to Google, and it is not part of the garden export on the “My garden” page; an import does not change it. After 12 months, or if we change the window, we ask again. If you allow, Google Analytics loads as described in the next section with only analytics storage granted; it sets cookies on your device (_ga, _ga_…), and data may be transferred to the USA. If you decline or later withdraw, measurement stops at once, Google Analytics is switched off for this site and the Google Analytics cookies (_ga, _ga_…) set for this domain are deleted. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for storing and reading information on your device § 165(3) Austrian Telecommunications Act 2021, TKG 2021).

Google’s consent management has priority: as soon as Google’s consent message is displayed, reports a decision or reports that the GDPR does not apply, only Google’s logic counts (also later during a visit); our window closes and is no longer shown, and a choice stored in it is deleted once Google reports a decision or that the GDPR does not apply. Whenever Google’s consent management refuses statistics, the Google Analytics cookies are deleted, even if you never used our window. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, Google Analytics stays off and the window is not shown. You can review, change or withdraw your choice at any time via at the bottom of every page, including this one. Pages without ads (the imprint, this privacy policy, the contact page, your garden page and the error page) load no Google code when they open; there the button opens our window so a choice made in it can be changed or withdrawn. Whenever you open the window via “Privacy settings”, it also offers “Open Google’s ad & U.S. privacy choices”, where you can change or withdraw your advertising consent or use the U.S. opt-out; this button never gives consent by itself. On pages with ads it opens Google’s consent management that is already loaded with the ad code. On pages without ads, Google’s consent script (from fundingchoicesmessages.google.com, without the ad code) is loaded only when you click it; Google’s decision then takes priority as described above, and Google Analytics still stays off on these pages.

For visitors in the U.S.: the window does not replace the U.S. opt-out. If Google’s consent management or the Global Privacy Control signal of your browser reports that you have opted out under a U.S. state privacy law, Google Analytics stays off and the window does not switch it on; where Google reports that the GDPR does not apply to your visit, the rules in the next section apply and the window is not shown.

Google Analytics 4

We use Google Analytics 4 (Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC, measurement ID G-T00HRG2YVF) to understand which puzzles and guides are used. Before any Google script runs, analytics is disabled. While Google’s consent message is not displayed, Google Analytics can also be allowed in our interim statistics window. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google Analytics is only loaded after you have consented in the consent message to storing and accessing information on a device (TCF purpose 1), to Google as a vendor and to analytics storage. Elsewhere it loads only if the consent message reports analytics as granted or not requiring consent, and reports that you have not opted out under a U.S. state privacy law; missing or unclear signals keep it off. When you withdraw consent via “Privacy settings”, analytics is switched off immediately. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, Google Analytics stays off. Google signals and ad personalization signals are disabled. Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. Data is kept for the retention period set in our Google Analytics property. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Transfers to Google LLC in the USA rely on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21). You can withdraw consent at any time with effect for the future. As we do not store personal data about visitors ourselves, most requests concern Google, whose controls are available at myaccount.google.com.

You have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. In Austria this is the Datenschutzbehörde, Barichgasse 40–42, 1030 Vienna, dsb@dsb.gv.at, www.dsb.gv.at; you can also contact the authority in your country of residence.

Notice for U.S. residents

Under some U.S. state privacy laws, such as the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, the use of advertising cookies for cross-context behavioral advertising can count as “selling” or “sharing” personal information. Where these laws apply, Google’s consent message offers an opt-out, which you can reopen via “Privacy settings”. We do not sell personal information in any other way and do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 16.
