The 31 techniques behind our puzzles, and how often boards need them

Our solver knows 31 techniques across the five puzzles. We ran it over the 150 daily boards of every puzzle and level – 2,250 boards from November 2, 2026 to March 31, 2027 – and counted every step. The table shows what each technique decides and how many boards needed it; the finder lists recent boards on which you can practice one.

The full list

A board’s level is set by the hardest technique its solution needs: Sprout boards are solved with Sprout techniques alone, Bloom boards need at least one Bloom technique, Grove boards at least one Grove technique. Each technique name links to its explanation.

What the counts show

The technique that gives a level its name is usually a small part of the board. A Grove Flower Beds board took 20.2 steps on average, and only 1.5 of them were the group technique; Touch-all was used 7.9 times per board. In Tents & Trees the look-ahead came up 2.2 times per Grove board, in Sun & Shade full-line counting 2.1 times. Garden Path is the exception: the stone-order argument was needed 9.1 times per Grove board.

Some techniques the solver knows were needed rarely or never. In all 450 boards of their puzzle: “Line check against crossing lines” in Sun & Shade (never); “Only way in” in Garden Path (2 of 450 boards); “No dead end” in Garden Path (never); “Either – or” in Who Planted What (never). The solver tries techniques in a fixed order from simple to advanced, so a later technique is recorded only when no earlier one applies; on these boards they were almost never the simplest available step. They remain part of the solver and can still appear in a hint.

Sprout really is one idea at a time. No Sprout board in the sample used a Bloom or Grove technique, and no Bloom board a Grove technique – the generator rejects boards that would.

How the counts were made

For every date and level, the build script generates the board exactly as your browser does, then follows the solver from the empty grid to the solution and tallies the technique of each step. The same path is what the walkthrough shows. Automatic consequences that the board displays for you – the dots around a planted flower, for example – are not steps.

Two limits matter when you read the numbers. First, they describe our solver’s route; a person may solve the same board with other steps. Second, when two techniques would both work, the simpler one is counted, so the figures for advanced techniques are lower bounds for how often they could be used. The sample is the first 150 days of the site, including dates that had not been played yet when this page was last built; the boards are fixed by their date, so the counts do not change.

Questions and answers

Why 150 days?

For the first 150 days after launch the site stores the generator’s starting point for the slowest boards, so all 2,250 boards can be rebuilt quickly and identically whenever the site is built. The automated tests recompute a sample and compare it with the stored counts.

Can I practice one technique on purpose?

Yes. Choose the puzzle and the technique in the finder above. It lists the boards of the last 30 days whose solution path uses that technique, with a link to play the board and a link that opens the walkthrough at the first step where the technique appears.

Do I need the advanced techniques to finish a Sprout board?

No. Every Sprout board in the sample was solved with Sprout techniques only; that is what the level means.

Are these the standard names for the techniques?

They are the names our hints use. Other puzzle sites and books use their own terms for similar ideas; where a guide of ours mentions a common name, it says so.
