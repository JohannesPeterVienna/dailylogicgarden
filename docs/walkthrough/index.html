Walkthrough: any daily board, one deduction at a time

Pick a puzzle, a level and a date. The walkthrough solves that board with the same solver that writes the Why? hints and shows every step on the board: which technique, which squares, and why. By default it opens yesterday’s board, so today’s puzzle is not given away.

How to use it

Next step and Back move through the solution; the arrow keys → and ← do the same, Home jumps to the start and End to the solved board. Squares with a solid outline are the ones the step reasons about, squares with a dashed outline are the ones it decides. Each step names its technique and links to the place where that technique is explained. The list under the board holds every step; select one to jump there.

Copy link to this step gives an address that opens exactly this board at this step – useful when you want to ask someone about one particular move. Copy all steps puts the whole path on the clipboard as plain text, and Print board and steps prints the empty board followed by the numbered list. Nothing is saved, and stepping through a board does not plant anything in your garden.

The walkthrough is not an answer key that was stored somewhere. Your browser rebuilds the board from the date, then asks the solver for the simplest available deduction again and again until the board is full. That is the same procedure our tests use to prove that a board can be solved without guessing.

What the walkthrough does not show

It shows one valid route, not the only one. The solver always takes the simplest technique that applies and, within a technique, the first place it finds in a fixed scanning order. You may well see a different step first; if it follows from the rules, it is just as right. The step count is therefore a property of our solver, not a score to beat.

The technique list is fixed and published on the technique reference, together with how often each one was needed on 150 days of boards.

Questions and answers

Does the walkthrough spoil today’s puzzle?

Only if you ask it to. It opens yesterday’s board unless the address names another date, and it shows a notice when the selected board is today’s.

Which dates can I open?

Every date from the launch on November 2, 2026 up to today (UTC). Boards from the last 30 days also have a “Play this board” button that opens them in the archive.

Why does my own solution use fewer or different steps?

Because the solver writes down one small deduction at a time and always prefers the simplest technique. A person often sees several of these at once or takes them in another order.

Is anything stored when I use it?

No. The walkthrough reads puzzle, level, date and step from the address and writes nothing to your browser’s storage. A copied link contains only those four values.
